### Goal no-overlap: spec and tasks (goal approved 2026-10-19; tasks await approval)

Decisions so far, from the user (2026-10-19): the later module is the **deeper one in the tree** (edges from the core); at equal depth the module reached through the later face in the order FRONT, BACK, RIGHT, LEFT, TOP, BOTTOM (at the first place the two paths differ) is the later one; the later module is removed with its whole subtree (the core is never removed). Collision detection is Trimesh with **python-fcl**. The repair is **opt-in**: a flag on `construct_mjspec_from_graph` (default off), so existing callers and examples keep their robots. The tolerance is **a percentage, size invariant, computed per pair on the go**: two modules overlap when the penetration depth is more than 1% of the thinnest side of the smaller of the two modules; parent and child modules are never compared. Each removal is reported with one debug log line. The goal runs after functional-notebooks and before pyright-zero (user's order).

Dependencies, to be approved again by name at the task that adds them (global rule: no download without approval): `python-fcl` 0.7.0.11 (PyPI, BSD, wheel for cp312 manylinux x86-64 4.66 MB; 2.0 MB macOS Intel, 1.57 MB macOS ARM, 1.1 MB Windows), `Cython` 3.3.0 (PyPI, wheel 3.41 MB on Linux x86-64; python-fcl lists it as a requirement with no marker), and `trimesh` 4.11.5 as a direct dependency (already locked and installed through `mujoco-mjx`, 0 MB new; `trimesh.collision.CollisionManager` raises "No FCL Available" today). `manifold3d` 3.4.1 is already a direct dependency of ARIEL and stays unused by this goal.

Time limit, answering the user's question: there is no runtime cutoff. The repair loops until no pair is deeper than the tolerance, and each round removes at least one module, so it ends after at most as many rounds as there are modules. A cutoff would make the robot depend on the speed of the machine. The 20 ms per body is a measured figure that goes in DESIGN.md Results; if it is far over, the next step is to speed the check up, not to stop it early.

Evidence (2026-10-19, MuJoCo contacts deeper than 0.1 mm between non-adjacent modules, bodies built by `construct_mjspec_from_graph`, 60 per source, seed 42): `random_tree(10)` 12 of 60, `random_tree(20)` 48 of 60, `HighProbabilityDecoder(20)` 25 of 60; building a body took 4 to 7 ms. `cppn_best_first.py` already avoids overlap at decode time with a grid of occupied cells; the others (hi-prob, vector, tree) do not.

Spec (proposed 2026-10-19; moves to DESIGN.md Specs once the user approves the tasks).

- Users: anyone who turns a graph into a robot with `construct_mjspec_from_graph` (the CPPN, probability, vector and tree decoders, the examples) and wants a body whose modules do not sit inside each other.
- Interface: `construct_mjspec_from_graph(graph, *, repair_overlaps=False)`. With the default nothing changes. With `True` the function returns the body of `repair_overlaps(graph)`, a new function in a new module `src/ariel/body_phenotypes/robogen_lite/overlap.py`, exported from `ariel.body_phenotypes`; it takes a `DiGraph` and returns a pruned copy (the argument is never changed).
- Geometry, read from the evidence (a 4-module body compiled 2026-10-19): every module geom in robogen-lite is a MuJoCo box (core half-size 0.1 m, brick 0.05 m, hinge stator 0.0248 x 0.0297 x 0.0248 and rotor 0.025 x 0.02 x 0.025), and a geom is named `<parent>-<child>-<face>-<kind>` (the core's geom is `core`), so the second number is the node it belongs to. A hinge has two geoms (stator, rotor), both owned by the hinge node. Any other geom type raises `ValueError` (none exists today).
- One round: build the body from the graph, compile it, run `mj_forward` for the world pose of every geom, make one Trimesh box per geom (extents twice the half-sizes, transform from the pose), put them in a `trimesh.collision.CollisionManager`, and ask for every colliding pair with contact data. A pair of geoms counts only when their owner nodes differ and are not parent and child in the graph. Its penetration depth is the largest depth among its contacts.
- Tolerance, per pair: the size of a module is the total volume of its geoms and its thinnest side is twice the smallest half-size of its geoms; the pair overlaps when `depth > 0.01 * thinnest side of the smaller module`. The ratio 0.01 is a named constant. Computed from the real sizes on every call, so scaling a body does not change the verdict.
- Later module of a violating pair: the one with more edges from the core; at equal depth, walk both paths from the core to the first edge where they differ and take the one whose face comes later in FRONT, BACK, RIGHT, LEFT, TOP, BOTTOM. The core is never removed (it is on every path, so it can never be the deeper of a pair).
- Loop: collect the violating pairs, take the one whose later module is deepest (ties: the smaller node id), remove that module with its whole subtree, log one debug line (`pruned 3 modules (ids 7, 9, 12) for overlap with 4`), and run a new round. Deepest first removes the least. It ends when a round finds no violating pair; every round removes at least one module, so it ends after at most as many rounds as there are modules.
- Not changed: poses at joint angle zero only; the decoders' own placement (`cppn_best_first` keeps its occupied-cell grid); genomes (the pruning acts on the graph made for one robot, the genome keeps its genes).
- Non-goals: overlap that appears when joints move; mesh or non-box geoms; regrowing pruned modules elsewhere; repair at genome level; turning the repair on by default or in the decoders; changing physics or module sizes; the `hardware` branch.
- Acceptance criteria and the test or measurement for each: (1) 0 of 60 bodies per source with a self-contact deeper than the tolerance after repair, counted by MuJoCo, not Trimesh (`tools/measure_overlap.py`, new, one command, seed 42; before the change the same tool must print today's counts); (2) a body without overlap gives byte-identical XML with the flag on and off (unit test on fixed graphs, plus the same check over the 180 bodies in the tool); (3) the later-module rule, the tolerance rule (a pair just under and just over 1%, the same pair scaled by 2) and the parent-child exemption each have a unit test on hand-built graphs; (4) the repair never changes its argument and always ends (unit tests); (5) repair time per body measured and recorded in DESIGN.md Results (target 20 ms, building a body takes 4 to 7 ms; not a pass or fail); (6) pytest coverage 100%, ruff, mypy unchanged in count.
